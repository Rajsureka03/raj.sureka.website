UXR CASE STUDY

SwallowCare: A Digital Care Companion for Adults with Dysphagia

Job Type

Sponsored-Project

ROLE

UX Researcher (Team Brunch)

TIMELINE

Semester-Long Project (5 Months)

METHODS

Semi-Structured Interviews, Survey, Competitive Analysis, Diary Study, Qualitative Analysis, Figma

1. Introduction

Overview: Dysphagia is a condition where individuals are experiencing difficulty with swallowing. SwallowCare is a mobile platform designed to provide educational support and access to community throughout user's care journey.

Problem Space

Prevalence of Dysphagia

Adults (50+) with dysphagia from stroke, cancer, or neurodegenerative recovery, and their caregivers.

Provider Education Barrier

Used primarily in home-care settings where specialized Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP) access is often fragmented.

Fragmentation in Solutions

Current digital solutions provide no cohesive experience, either lacking critical features or only focusing on one aspect of the education process.

Problem Statement

  • How might we build a more centralized platform for adults living with dysphagia so they can gain access to the educational resources, community support, and the care providers that they need to streamline their care journey?
2. Problem Framing & Goals

Key Research Questions

  • How do patients' care paths differ based on their condition's cause?
  • Where are the current barriers in education, diagnosis, and progress tracking for patients, caregivers, and providers?
  • How can we support and motivate patients while maintaining their safety and privacy?
3. Methodology & Data Collection

  • Participants: Conducted semi-structured interviews with 5 Speech Language-Pathologists (SLPs), 4 Patients, 1 ENT Physician, 1 Dietitian.

4. Key Research Insights

Our research identified critical gaps in personalization, digital tracking, and community support across the patient care continuum.

Core Finding
Supporting Evidence
Resource Scarcity + Poor Visibility
Patients face difficulties with receiving a diagnosis
59.3% of surveyed patients experienced challenges with receiving a diagnosis
Lack of Centralized Educational Support
Patients experienced a lack of support and access to available educational resources
Users primarily utilized organizational websites such as NFOSD to obtain educational resource support
Fragmentation in Care Journey
Patients need to hop between apps to access the care support that they need
..Currently, no applications exist that target the entire dysphagia care journey
Need for Personalized Care
Patient's dysphagia type and severity determine their rehabilitation, exercises, and diet regiment
..Our survey shows that stroke, surgery, and dementia are the most prevalent conditions

INSIGHT A: Resources are Not Available for Patients

There is a lack of available resources and visibility for patients to receive the care and support that they need when receiving a diagnosis and getting access to treatment

INSIGHT B: Community Support is Lacking for Patients

Patients are not able to get access to the adequate support that they need, and each individual requires varying levels of support and resources with their condition.

INSIGHT C: Patients Exhibit Poor Health Literacy

Overall, patients are not well educated about their condition. Currently, patients are experiencing gaps in their comprehension of the physiological responses that are occurring within their body

INSIGHT D: Current Solutions Provide Incomplete Care

Current solutions only address a small portion of the user's care journey, forcing them to bounce between different applications to receive the care and support that they need

Artifact: User Needs & Design Implications

Based on our research, the design of SwallowCare must address three core thematic challenges, linking each one to specific user needs and the resulting design implications.

Research Finding 1: Infrastructure Limitations

Support groups lack discoverability despite them having a variety of benefits and applications

→ ↓

Design Implication

Patients must be able to find a way to connect with others that are part of a similar community

Current methods of symptom tracking and telemedicine support are limited due to fragmentation in the user's journey

→ ↓

Design Implication

The design should provide users with the ability to track their therapy and the progress of their condition

Research Finding 2: Treatment Difficulties

Dysphagia timelines and treatment differ based on their underlying cause

→ ↓

Design Implication

The design must be personalized to adhere to different treatment timelines based on the patient's underlying cause

Patients face challenges with treatment due to a lack of access of available SLPs in their area

→ ↓

Design Implication

Users in areas where there is limited access to providers should still be able to access the necessary care that they need

Telehealth treatment is effective but diagnosis must be completed in person

→ ↓

Design Implication

Users should be able to have access to providers who can schedule in-person appointments so they can receive a diagnosis

Research Finding 3: Patient-Motivated Challenges

Patients face difficulties with staying motivated and adhering to diet restrictions during treatment

→ ↓

Design Implication

Accountability should be built into the design for patients to adhere to their dietary restrictions

Patients face challenges with understanding their condition due to a lack of educational resources and with locating adequate support

→ ↓

Design Implication

Patients and providers should be able to view the same symptom-related information in a unified and cohesive way

7. Final High-Fidelity Design

Final SwallowCare Experience

Our high-fidelity Figma prototype consists of four major flows: Onboarding, Exercise Companion, Diet/Symptom Logging, and an AI Assistant.

01. Personalization

Onboarding

Onboards users onto the application and collects patient data during the onboarding process

02. Therapy Support

Exercise Logging

Provides patients with real-time guidance to complete their swallowing exercise.

03. Symptom Tracking

Diet & Symptom Logging

Simple symptom and diet tracking for users to easily track their food and nutrient consumption. The design incorporated large touch targets to cater to users with limited motor skills and visual challenges

04. AI Assistant

AI-Powered Assistance

An AI-powered assistant that provides personalized guidance and support to users throughout their therapy journey.

8. Final Evaluation & Key Findings

Evaluation Techniques

Our team evaluated our high-fidelity prototypes with industry experts and primary stakeholders.

Cognitive Walkthrough

My researcher and I had UX designers and Speech Language Pathologists evaluate our designs using the Nielson Norman cognitive walkthrough key questions.

Usability Testing

Utilizing UserTesting.com, my researcher and I recruited individuals diagnosed with a swallowing disorder to obtain user feedback on our solution

Cognitive Walkthrough

75% Lacked Visibility

Identified that 3 out of 4 core features lacked clear visual cues, requiring features to become more easily discoverable.

Usability Evaluation

60% Satisfaction Rate

Overall, users enjoyed utilizing the platform and found the features and education content beneficial for their conditions.

Key Findings from Evaluation

✓

Feature Visibility

Users noted that some of the features, specifically the "Food Logging" feature was not easily visible.

✓

Verified Sources

Users liked that the information from the "Learn Library" originated from verified sources (Cleveland Clinic).

✓

Visual and Auditory Accessibility

Users noted that the audio feature was beneficial since our primary user group is older users (individuals 50 and older).

!

Product Development / Functional Prototype

Users expressed frustration due to a restricted prototype, preventing them from inputting their symptoms if their condition wasn't listed as one of the pre-listed conditions.
