3. Methodology & Key Findings

Methodology: Usability Testing & Ideation

For the OtO Lawn, Ace Last Mile and Scotts and AeroGarden subscription projects, my team and I conducted usability testing (N=6 participants each) via UserTesting. The Ortho project utilized low-fidelity prototyping from the insights that were obtained from meeting the Ortho brand and in-store teams.

Usability Test Protocol

  • Planning: Used Whimsical to structure tasks, test scenarios, and map user flow expectations (OtO, Ace, Subscription).
  • Execution: Launched tests on UserTesting platform to observe user behavior and gather spoken feedback.

Ideation & Design

  • Process: Brainstorming, user flow diagramming, and creating low-fidelity sketches (Ortho Problem Solver).
  • Goal: Define an intuitive, regionalized solution for pest/weed identification and product recommendation.

Detailed plan of our usability protocol for gaining customer insight into the Delivery by Ace CTA on Scotts.com's product detail pages

Key Finding: Ace Last Mile CTA Usability

Pricing and Page Handoff Confusion

  • Mixed Reactions on Pricing: Participants expected transparent, exact pricing and were confused why the price differed slightly from Scotts.com to the Ace checkout page.
  • Unnecessary Step: Some users felt the forced page transition to Ace was an extra, unnecessary step, especially if there was a minimal price difference between Scotts.com's price and Ace's checkout price.

Detailed plan of our usability protocol and the tasks and questions that we were interested in our participants completing

Key Finding: OtO Lawn Website Clarity

High Interest, High Concern

  • Positive Impressions : Users immediately liked the OtO device concept (automatic, weather-connected watering).
  • Primary Concern: Users with large lawns were concerned about coverage area and whether they would need to purchase multiple devices (suggesting a clarity issue around product capability).
4. Ortho Problem Solver: Design & Ideation

The Ortho Problem Solver project aimed to digitize the in-store experience, providing consumers with a robust, educational solution for pest and weed issues via a simple QR scan.

Artifact: Ideation Flow (In-Store Signage)

The posted QR code will be displayed in home improvement stores in the pest-related aisle to capture customers as they are shopping

Shoppers can scan the QR code to access the Ortho Quiz to learn more about their specific pest problem and find the best solution for them

Design Requirements & Value

The digital solution was designed to achieve three main business values:

  • Capture and Retain: Capture more consumers shopping in the aisle and retain existing shoppers who are searching for pest-related products.
  • Trusted Solutions: Consumers receive recommended solutions from a trusted, verified brand.
  • Education: Consumers are educated on their specific pest/weed problems to tackle current issues and prevent future problems.

Core Feature Mockups

Insect Seeker: Flow guiding users to identification based on pest attributes (e.g., number of wings, region).

Garden Seeker: Regionalized recommendations for prevalent weed and lawn challenges.

5. Key Takeaways & Next Steps

Lessons Learned & Future Directions

  • Be Intentional and Proactive: It is extremely important, especially in research to be intentional and to ask the right questions. I should have been more proactive with testing our protocol and making sure there were no flaws in our protocol test as this would have saved us time in the long run
  • Leverage Your Network: During my internship, there were projects that required the support and input from cross-functional partners. Had I been more proactive and consulted with the stakeholders early on into the process, I would not have wasted time receiving feedback or advice that deterred from the scope or goals of the project
