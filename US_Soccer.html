UXR CASE STUDY | U.S. SOCCER FEDERATION

A Unified Pickup Platform for Soccer Fan Engagement

Job Type

Sponsored-Project

ROLE

UX Researcher (Team Brunch)

TIMELINE

Semester-Long Project (5 Months)

METHODS

Observation, User Interviews, Low-Fidelity Prototyping

1. Introduction

Overview: Soccer is only the 7th most popular sport in the US cultivated by its deep roots to individuals' culture and experiences.

Problem Space: My team and I partnered with the US Soccer Federation to identify ways to increase access and decrease barriers of entry for prospective pick up soccer players to join teams and leagues.

Problem Statement

  • How might we create a more engaged soccer community by improving the pickup discovery experience through a structured and unified platform at the individual and community level?
2. Problem Framing & Goals

Our research identified a key insight: U.S.-based fans often become fans through playing the sport. How can we lower the barrier to entry for playing and keep amateur players engaged?

Key Research Questions

  • How might we incorporate soccer culture into the local fan experience in a way that feels authentic and deepens fan's appreciation and understanding of the sport itself?
  • How might we create a digitalized community that caters to the unique identities and experiences within the diverse sub-community groups of soccer fans
  • How might we create personalized educational content for users to help them transition from casual spectators to knowledgable experts as their engagment and comprehension of soccer grows?

Phase 2: Synthesis and Thematic Analysis

Research Objectives

Identify the friction points in the transition from "viewer" to "player" and determine how social connectivity influences game frequency.

Key Synthesis Findings

  • • Culture: Soccer culture varies wildly by region, affecting rule interpretation.
  • • Barriers: Newcomers feel "locked out" of existing private group chats.
  • • Lifecycle: Engagement drops immediately after a match ends due to lack of a "third space."
  • • Discovery: Most players find games via word-of-mouth, not digital platforms.

Core Finding: Pickup is an untapped area because the current system requires high social capital, creating barriers for newcomers.

4. Key Research Insights

Our analysis identified that the solution must be unified and structured to solve the chaos inherent in amateur play.

INSIGHT A: Logistical Barrier to Entry

Newcomers and non-experts face immense difficulty joining games due to unstructured logistics (time, location, rulebooks, RSVP management) which are not streamlined.

"No streamlined way for newcomers to easily join soccer leagues through simple sign-up processes, clear rules, and easy-to-find game times and locations."

INSIGHT B: Community Fragmentation

Existing pickup groups operate in isolation, lacking formal structure and tools to connect with other local teams or facilitate long-lasting internal relationships.

"Balancing the need for intimacy within private sub-communities with one that fosters discovery between different local groups."

INSIGHT C: Lack of Post-Game Motivation

Engagement ends after the game, leaving no narrative or incentive (digital or otherwise) to maintain individual performance or loyalty to the community.

"Beginner pick up soccer players need to experience a measurable, continuous feeling of achievement that grounds local amateur play"

Core User Requirements

A Successful Unified Pickup Platform MUST:

  • 1. Lower Barrier for Comprehension + Entry

    Bring simplicity to pickup-related logistics (time, location, rulebooks, RSVP, etc.).

  • 2. Facilitate Connection in Sub-Communities

    Create and maintain long-lasting relationships within a league and improve discovery between local leagues.

  • 3. Provide Info Pathway Personalization

    Engage users by providing the appropriate amount and type of information tailored to their skill level and background.

  • 4. Individual Playing, Tracking + Achievement

    Track player's performance and award digital badges to maintain motivation and ensure individual recognition.

  • Helping new fans discover and understand soccer.
  • Improving the experience for existing fans.
  • Making playing soccer more accessible.
  • Engaging local businesses around the sport of soccer.

User Feedback Session

Before proceeding to high-fidelity design, usability testing was conducted on the low-fidelity prototype to validate core assumptions, identify pain points, and iterate on the functional flow, particularly around Match Finding and Team Coordination.

Key Finding 1: More Clarity in Proposing Pickup Matches (Shall We Play) vs. Playing Pickup Matches (Let's Play)

Users requested more clarity in understanding the differences between the Let's Play (joining an open, immediate game) feature and Shall We Play (challenging an existing team for a future game) features.

Key Finding 2: Mental Burden in Utilizing the Sidebar and Footer Navigation and Memorizing Feature Locations

The current navigation structure requires that users memorize the locations of specific features within the application and with having to address both the sidebar and footer navigations.

Key Finding 3: Lack of Incorporating Real-world Operational Constraints to Support Match Day Play

We need to address incorporating real-world constraints within our pickup soccer application such as with choosing to book referees or with renting out soccer fields.

10. Reflection & Next Steps

Lessons Learned

  • Discovery Research: During our inital recruitment stage, our team experienced challenges with recruitment participants and finding field sites to conduct our direct and indirect observations. So, we got creative. We attended Georgia Tech Football Games and visited International Soccer Bars and Restaurants in Atlanta to observe fan engagement and fan behaviors during our observation sessions.
  • Skill Development: Despite having a limited background and experience in research and design, I was able to gain hands-on experience with conducting interviews and with observing our target user group in their natural environment. Additionally, I was able to apply the skills and information that I have been learning from my Design Foundations class when creating some of the initial sketches for our pick up soccer prototype.

Future Directions (Next Steps)

  • Game Discovery: Improve the game discovery experience for spontaneous games through a better scheduling process.
  • Design Changes: The layout should differentiate different upcoming matches between individual, team, and league discovery
